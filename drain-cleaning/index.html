Drain Cleaning in Round Rock, TX

Clearing slow, gurgling, backed-up, and odorous drains across Round Rock.

Call (737) 405-5790 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles after the faucet stops, a shower that holds water around your feet, or a floor drain with a sewer smell usually points to a blockage somewhere beyond the fixture. We check the basin, trap, drain opening, and visible pipe route to tell whether the restriction is close to the room or farther along the line.

Hair, soap residue, grease, food scraps, and paper can collect in different parts of a drain pipe. We clear the blockage without treating every slow fixture as the same problem, because a bathroom sink, kitchen line, and floor drain can each have a different route and access point. A mildly slow sink may wait briefly if water still drains, but repeated backup, a floor drain filling, or wastewater rising in a tub should not wait because the blockage can overflow into the room and spread farther through the pipe.

Gurgling sinks and floor drains

We often hear about a kitchen sink that drains slowly until the dishwasher runs, a shower pan that fills before the water is shut off, or a toilet that makes nearby sink water bubble. A gurgling sound can mean air is being displaced by water around a restriction, while standing water in a tub may indicate that the blockage is lower in the shared branch pipe.

We also get calls about a rotten smell from a floor drain, water coming back through a laundry drain, and a basin that empties only after plunging. Grease at a kitchen trap, hair around a stopper, scale inside an older pipe, or debris farther down the line can produce similar symptoms. We inspect the fixture and pipe route before recommending work, since these faults can look alike from the room but require different equipment, access, and cost.

Drain inspection and clearing

We start the visit by running water at the affected fixture, checking the stopper or strainer, and looking at the trap, cleanout, and exposed drain pipe. A basic fixture check commonly takes about 10 to 15 minutes. Sink and tub branches are often smaller than a main drain, so we match the cable and cutting head to the opening and pipe rather than forcing a large tool through a narrow fixture line.

Where the blockage is reachable, we can usually remove debris from a stopper, clean a trap, or cable-clear a branch on the same visit; a typical fixture-level cable clearing may take 30 to 60 minutes depending on the buildup and pipe bends. If flow does not return, we may use a camera to look for roots, a break, heavy scale, or an offset joint farther along the route. A camera inspection can add another 30 to 60 minutes, while work requiring access behind a wall, beneath a slab, or farther outside the building is normally arranged as a separate visit. If a damaged strainer, stopper, trap washer, or cleanout cap is found, we explain the part before replacement.

Pipe route and cleanout access

The pipe route, the access point, and the pipe material are major factors in the scope of drain work. A clog at a sink trap under a cabinet is different from a blockage reached through a floor cleanout, roof vent, or exterior access point. PVC, cast iron, galvanized steel, and older mixed pipe runs can each affect how we approach the line and which cable head or camera setup is appropriate.

Heavy grease, compacted paper, tree roots, mineral scale, and a collapsed section do not clear in the same way. Cabinets packed around a trap, a painted-over cleanout cap, or a cap that has seized can add preparation and may require a replacement fitting. We also account for whether the affected drain serves a single fixture or several rooms, and whether access through a tenant space, utility room, or exterior gate needs approval before the line can be checked.

Fixture and cleanout location

Before calling, note which fixture is slow or backed up and which room it is in, such as the kitchen sink, hall bathroom tub, laundry standpipe, or garage floor drain. Tell us whether water is standing now, whether another fixture bubbles when water runs, and whether the problem began after grease, wipes, food, or another item entered the drain.

If you can safely see a cleanout cap, let us know whether it is under a sink, in a utility room, outside near the foundation, or at a floor drain. A photo of the sink cabinet, trap, or access opening can help us plan the cable and camera equipment. Avoid pouring chemical cleaner into the pipe before we arrive, since the liquid can remain in the trap and create a hazard around the drain opening.
