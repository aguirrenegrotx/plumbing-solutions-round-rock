Drain Cleaning in Round Rock, TX

We clear slow, noisy, backed-up, and odor-producing drains in Round Rock.

Call (737) 405-5790 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles after it drains, a tub that holds a ring of water, or a floor drain with a sewer smell usually means water is meeting resistance somewhere in the pipe. We check the fixture, trap, branch line, and available cleanout to locate whether the restriction is close to the drain opening or farther along the waste line.

Grease can collect in a kitchen branch, hair and soap residue can narrow a shower line, and wipes or paper can stop a toilet branch even when the bowl itself still flushes. A blockage may also affect more than one fixture: running the washing machine might make water rise in a nearby tub, or an upstairs sink may cause a downstairs drain to gurgle.

A slow single sink can sometimes wait until it becomes a repeat problem, provided water is still draining and there is no wastewater on the floor. A backup, sewer odor, water rising in another fixture, or waste coming through a floor drain should not wait, because a drain blockage can send wastewater into cabinets, tubs, and low areas of the home.

Gurgling drains and sewer odor

Customers often call us about a kitchen sink that drains slowly, a shower pan that fills around their feet, or a toilet that bubbles when the washing machine empties. Other common descriptions are “water comes up in the tub,” “the floor drain smells like sewage,” and “the sink makes a glugging sound after I turn off the faucet.” Each description points us toward a different portion of the drain system.

A single slow bathroom sink can be caused by buildup in the stopper, tailpiece, or trap. A tub that backs up when the toilet flushes can indicate a restriction in the shared branch line, while a sewer odor near a floor drain may come from a dry trap, a vent issue, or waste held in the pipe. Water around a cleanout cap or at the base of a cabinet can also mean the line is blocked beyond the fixture connection.

These symptoms can look alike from the room, but the pipe route, blockage material, and access point can be completely different. We inspect the drain before recommending work because a nearby trap blockage and a deeper line obstruction are separate faults with different labor and equipment needs.

Drain line inspection

At the callout, we start with the affected fixture and ask which drains are slow, which appliances were running when the problem appeared, and whether water has backed up elsewhere. We run water where appropriate, watch the drain level, listen for gurgling, and check the trap, drain opening, cabinet piping, floor drain, and accessible cleanout. A lavatory line is commonly one and one-quarter inches in diameter, while many kitchen, tub, and shower branches use one-and-one-half- or two-inch pipe; the pipe size affects the cable and cutting head we use.

We remove reachable hair, soap residue, food buildup, or debris at the drain opening and use drain-cleaning equipment through the correct access point when the obstruction is farther into the line. A straightforward sink, tub, or shower blockage may take roughly thirty to ninety minutes once we can reach the line. If the drain remains slow after clearing, we may need to inspect farther along the route to identify a damaged pipe, root intrusion, a shifted joint, or buildup beyond the branch.

Most accessible fixture and branch-line blockages can be addressed during the same visit. A buried line, a blocked cleanout, pipe damage behind a wall, or a route that requires excavation is assessed first and scheduled separately so the required access and repair work can be planned correctly.

Pipe access and blockage depth

The location of the blockage matters because a restriction at a sink trap is reached differently from one beyond a wall, under a slab, or out in the yard. An exposed cleanout near the affected line gives us a direct route into the pipe, while a cabinet full of stored items, a sealed access panel, or no visible cleanout can add preparation and diagnostic work.

Pipe material and condition also affect the work. Older cast iron can hold scale along its interior, galvanized pipe can narrow from corrosion, and plastic pipe may have tight fittings or long turns that limit how equipment travels. Grease, hair, paper, mineral buildup, and tree roots each call for a different approach, and recurring stoppages can point to a defect rather than a one-time clog.

The drain route is another factor. A blockage near the fixture generally takes less tracing than one affecting several rooms or a main line, especially when wastewater appears at the lowest drain in the building. If inspection shows a cracked section, a separated joint, or a pipe that needs replacement, we explain the access needed before that additional work is arranged.

Cleanout location and fixture

Before calling, note which room has the slow or backed-up drain and which fixture starts the problem first. Tell us whether the kitchen sink, shower, tub, toilet, washing machine standpipe, or floor drain is involved, and whether another drain reacts when water is used.

If you know where the cleanout is, let us know whether it is in a bathroom, garage, exterior wall, crawl space, or yard. Clear items from under the affected sink or around the floor drain if it is safe to do so, and mention any water that is currently standing in a tub, sink, or shower pan.

It also helps to describe what went into the drain before the stoppage, such as grease, coffee grounds, wipes, hair, or a small object. Call Plumbing Solutions Round Rock at (737) 405-5790 before a backup turns into wastewater coming through a tub, floor drain, or cabinet drain.
